# AI smells

<!-- Append-only. Fragments separated by `---` (writing-fragments format); newest
at the bottom. The structural tells of machine-written prose — many caught,
honestly, in my own drafts. -->

**THE FALSE INVERSION.** `You do not {{not_concept}}. You {{concept}}.`

The shape promises a reversal and frequently delivers a plain definition. It
manufactures a turn by first denying a thing nobody claimed, so the assertion
that follows feels earned. I wrote it straight-faced in the washe beats: "You do
not bind routes to handlers. You write a command." Sometimes the inversion is
real and load-bearing. The smell is that the template is *available* whether or
not the content is — the cadence fires on reflex.

---

**THE CHIASTIC KOAN.** `The {{noun}} is not {{verb_phrase}}. It is {{verb_phrase}}.`

Two verb phrases, one negated, a beat of silence, and the reader is invited to
feel insight. "The route table is not configured. It is discovered." "A tool that
thinks in pages is not wrong about washe. It is undefined on washe." It reads as
depth because of the rhythm, not because a claim was made — the music does the
work the argument should. The deletion test catches it: strike the "not" clause.
If the second sentence still lands on its own, the first was only staging.

---

**THE FLOGGED METAPHOR.** A figure reached for where the plain word was already
clearer, then repeated until the repetition passes for structure.

"There is one source of truth — the registry — and everything else is a shadow it
casts. The routes are a shadow. The help is a shadow. The tab-completion is a
shadow. The address bar is a shadow." The word underneath is *derived*, and
*derived* was the stronger word. Worse, the figure is slightly false: a shadow is
a dimmed, lossy projection, and I meant lossless derivation — so the metaphor does
not merely decorate, it smuggles a wrong implication, and then the anaphora drives
the wrong implication in four times. Two tests: replace the figure with the
literal term — if the sentence gets clearer, the figure was detracting. And pull
the figure out from under the anaphora — if the repetition collapses without it,
it was rhythm wearing the costume of argument.

---

**THE FALSE INVERSION, hyperbolic cut.** (a sub-species of THE FALSE INVERSION,
above.) `You never {{verb_phrase}} in {{hyperbolic_timeframe}}. You {{verb}} {{noun}} and {{concept}}.`

The false inversion with a lifetime bolted on — the denial reaches for "never,"
"in your life," "not once," to inflate the reveal. Mine, verbatim: "You never
designed a route in your life. You wrote a command and let it be addressable." The
hyperbole is the tell: the timeframe does emotional work the claim cannot back — I
have no idea what the reader designed across their life and do not need to, yet the
sentence wants the swagger of certainty about a stranger's whole career. Cut the
timeframe and the claim survives, smaller and true. The smell is the reach for
"never … ever," not the inversion underneath it.

---

**THE FOIL FIRST.** Introduce a thing by defining its incumbent and saying what
the new thing is *not*, so the reader carries the old model plus a negation before
ever meeting the positive identity.

Mine: "The DOM is a tree you build and the browser remembers. washe builds no
tree. It writes cells into a grid — `setCell(x, y, char, fg, bg, attrs)` —
composes the whole frame, and paints it." The DOM gets the topic sentence; washe
arrives as "builds no tree"; the positive — *writes cells into a grid* — is the
third beat, behind a foil and a negation. Elements Rule 10: negation is not
identity. State what it is — "washe writes cells into a grid and paints the whole
frame" — and the reader derives the contrast with the DOM for free, faster than I
could stage it. Keep a foil only where the reader would otherwise assume the
incumbent, and never as the opening move. (The tail of that same passage — "Zero
calls to `document.createElement`. Not few. Zero." — is the reflex again at clause
scale: name the low strawman just to negate it. The number alone was the point.)
